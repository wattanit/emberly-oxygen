# Emberly Oxygen 0.4.1 — Release note

Nov 11, 2026 · @Wattanit

**One technique: threads.** The matrix products and the attention now run on a pool of worker threads. The output is bit-identical at every thread count, the default is 4 threads, and on this machine prompts run up to 5.6× faster and generation up to 1.65× faster than on one thread. Built against Requirements 0.9 and Technical Specification 0.7 (both approved on 2026-11-11). Versioning note: the optimisation series after 0.4.0 is patch-numbered by the owner's decision (0.4.1 threads, 0.4.2 next).

## What changed

- **A pool of persistent worker threads** (FR-43, FR-44; `pool.rs`). Workers are `std::thread`s created when the model loads and kept waiting; a job is an `Arc`ed closure over a range of output elements and its result comes back over a channel, so nothing borrowed crosses a thread and the engine still has no `unsafe` beyond the memory-map call in the loader. The calling thread takes the first range itself; a count of 1 makes no workers; 0 is rejected naming `threads`; workers are joined when the model drops; a panic in a worker reaches the caller.
- **Threaded products.** `matvec` and `matmul` divide the weight's rows into contiguous ranges, one per thread. Each output element is computed whole, by the same 16-partial-sum dot product as before, so no sum is split and no result changes. Products under 500,000 multiply-adds (the key and value projections, the 576-row products) stay on the calling thread, since a hand-over costs more than they take.
- **Threaded attention.** In a decode step the query heads are divided among threads (from 256 positions of context; below that the hand-over costs about what it saves). In the batched prompt pass the rows are divided, taken in a zigzag order (first row, last row, second row, ...) so each thread gets a mix of cheap early rows and dear late ones. The key-value cache's per-layer vectors are shared (`Arc`) and workers drop their handles before answering, so the next append extends the vector in place.
- **`--threads N`** on `generate` and `chat`, and `Model::load_with_threads(dir, n)`. The default is 4 (fewer on a smaller machine), chosen by the sweep below. `-v` reports the count. 0 is rejected before any work, naming `threads` and the value.
- **A thread sweep in the benchmark** (FR-45): 1, 2, 4, 8, 12 and 16 threads, the prompt at 128 and 644 tokens and 128 generated tokens after 32 and 512 tokens of context, after checking every count gives exactly one thread's last logits. Every row of every group now carries `threads`.
- **A negative control for the division of work**, `SkippedRows`: with 4 threads, the last range of every threaded product is left at zero. The harness fails on it (logits and intermediates).
- **The test suite grew from 420 to 452 tests.**

## What was measured

Apple M4 Max (12 performance and 4 efficiency cores), commit `6c9fab4` (clean tree, version 0.4.1, straight after 452 tests passed).

**The output is the same, at every count.** On all 12 prompts the logits and every intermediate are exactly equal at 2, 3, 7 and 16 threads to the one-thread values, through the batched pass; one token at a time, on the shortest and longest prompt, at 1, 2, 3, 7 and 16. The oracle's continuation and a greedy chat reply are identical at every count tried. Every category passes S-1 to S-4, S-9, S-10 and S-11 with the same worst errors as 0.4.0 (3.50e-4 in logits, 9.97e-6 relative in intermediates). Counts 3 and 7 divide none of the model's sizes evenly, on purpose.

**Against 0.4.0's rows at the default 4 threads:** the short workload's prompt throughput 169.2 → **509.2 tok/s (3.01×)**, time to first token 189.1 → **62.9 ms**, generation 115.2 → **166.3 tok/s (+44%)**; the long workload's prompt throughput 137.2 → **314.2 tok/s (+129%)**, time to first token 3730.7 → **1629.5 ms**, generation 82.0 → **134.3 tok/s (+64%)**. A chat turn's time to first token is **0.15 s at 67 tokens of history (0.42 s before) and 2.1 s at 644 (4.9 s before)**. Peak memory is unchanged at 811.7 MB.

## What was learned

- **Creating threads per call would have made the engine slower.** Task 20.1 measured it before any pool was built: a scoped thread costs about 8 µs per extra thread at every call (122 µs at 16), more than the whole of most products; a persistent worker's hand-over costs about 3 µs per extra worker. Only the unembedding and the 1536-row products repay it at small counts.
- **Safe code was enough.** The worry was how long-lived workers could read a cache that grows every step without `unsafe`. Sharing each layer's vectors by `Arc`, copying the small input per call and returning each worker's output over a channel works, and the calling structure (every call collects every answer before it returns) makes the append that follows free of copies.
- **Decode is limited by the number of hand-overs, not by threads.** Read bandwidth rises 4× at 12 threads in the profile, but decode gains at most 1.67×. A step makes about 150 threaded hand-overs at 10 to 25 µs each, which is 1.5 to 4 ms of a 7.3 ms step by the task 20.1 costs. This is consistent with the measurements, not measured in the engine.
- **A control for a division of work needs workers to lose.** `SkippedRows` does nothing at one thread, so its tests run at an explicit 4.

## Negative results and open points

- **The default is the rule's, and it leaves prompt speed unused.** The rule of Specification §12 (highest decode speed at 512 tokens, ties within 2% to the smaller) picks 4 threads. 8 threads is 0.9% faster in decode and 12 threads gives 2.4× the prompt speed of 4 (883.8 against 2131.0 ms at 644 tokens). A different rule, or separate counts for prompt and decode, is the owner's to decide; the count is one flag away.
- **Decode gains 1.65× at best and gets slower again beyond 8 threads.** Fusing the products that share an input into one hand-over, and attention being limited to 9 heads, are the candidates; neither was tried.
- **Two threads gain little on the prompt** (1.24× at 128 tokens) and 4 gain 2.3×; not traced. Candidates: the input copy and the transposed output copy per product, and unequal work in equal row ranges.
- **Not measured: another machine.** The rows rest on one machine. The bit-identity of the output rests on dividing only whole output elements; a later technique that splits a sum would give it up.
- **Not threaded:** normalization, RoPE, SiLU, the elementwise operations and sampling stay on the calling thread (Requirements §13).
