# Emberly Oxygen 0.4.2 — Release note

Nov 12, 2026 · @Wattanit

**One technique: register blocking.** The batched matrix-matrix product, which the prompt pass spends about 80% of its time in, now computes a tile of output elements at a time in registers, with each product added by a fused multiply-add, in safe code. On one thread a 128-token prompt takes 332 ms against 806 ms in 0.4.1; at the default thread count the long workload's time to first token falls from 730 ms to 428 ms, and a chat turn at 644 tokens of history from 1.03 s to 0.61 s. Decode is unchanged. This is the first release that gives up bit-identity with the previous one on purpose: the float64 reference judges it, and its error against the reference is lower than 0.4.1's. Built against Requirements 0.10 and Technical Specification 0.8 (both approved on 2026-11-12). Patch-numbered in the optimisation series (0.4.1 threads, 0.4.2 register blocking).

## What changed

- **A register-blocked matrix-matrix product** (`matmul_blocked`, FR-46). A tile of 4 weight rows by 16 inputs is held in registers; the inputs are packed once per call into `[k][16]` panels so the inner loop reads 16 consecutive inputs per weight value; the weights are read in place, with no packed copy. Every element is a sum over the shared dimension in index order, taken in chunks of 64 products (each chunk with `f32::mul_add`, rounded once per product, the chunk sums added in order). Leftover rows and inputs are summed one element at a time in the same order, so an element's value depends on neither the tile it falls in nor how rows are divided among threads. Safe code: no `unsafe`, no SIMD intrinsics.
- **`prefill` uses it** for every projection, and for the unembedding when all positions' logits are asked for. The decode step, the unembedding of the last position, the attention, sampling and chat are unchanged. The pool divides tile groups among threads (`matmul_blocked_pooled`).
- **The 0.4.1 product stays** in the library as the baseline of the benchmark and of the drift test (`Model::use_unblocked_prefill`, behind the `bench-kernels` feature, which a normal build never enables).
- **A new equality rule** (FR-47). The oracle judges the release with the same thresholds as before. Differences from the previous kernel are bounded and reported: blocked against unblocked `prefill`, and batched against one token at a time, may differ by up to 1e-3 in logits (the worst measured is 3.53e-4). Output still does not depend on the thread count, exactly (the test compares 1, 2, 3, 7 and the default), and is the same from run to run.
- **A negative control**, `UnfinishedTile`: the last, partial tile of every blocked product is left unwritten. The harness fails on it, and so does the drift test.
- **The benchmark** has batched kernel rows (the 0.4.1 loop, the blocked kernel and `matrixmultiply`'s sgemm in GFLOP/s at the three prompt shapes, checked against the kernel's definition before timing), and its prefill sweep accepts the two paths' new rounding difference. Every row still records its thread count.
- **The test suite grew from 452 to 463 tests.**

## What was measured

Apple M4 Max, commit `bb57d55` (clean tree, version 0.4.2, straight after 463 tests passed). Three benchmark runs were taken, and two of them are in the record: the first stopped on the prefill sweep's agreement check (see below) and recorded nothing; the second, at the then default of 8 threads, picked 12 by the sweep's rule, so the default was set to 12 and the rows retaken, which is the third.

**The output is judged by the reference, and its error went down.** Every category passes S-1 to S-4, S-9, S-10 and S-11 with unchanged thresholds. Worst error against the 64-bit reference: **2.72e-4 in logits (27% of S-1; 0.4.1: 3.50e-4)** and **7.98e-6 relative in intermediates (8% of S-2; 0.4.1: 9.97e-6)**. The ids of 30 recorded runs (greedy and seeded generation on all 12 prompts, greedy and seeded chat over three turns) are identical to 0.4.1's; that is a measurement, not a promise (FR-47). Blocked and unblocked `prefill` differ by at most 3.53e-4 in logits on every position of every prompt; the sweep's two paths differ by about 1.7e-5 in the last logits.

## What was learned

- **The fused multiply-add was the difference, and it is safe code.** The first safe blocked kernel, with a separate multiply and add, reached 65 GFLOP/s, 1.75× the old loop, and looked like the end of what safe code could do against sgemm's 110. Rust does not fuse a multiply and an add by itself; `f32::mul_add` compiles to the hardware instruction on this machine, and the same tile then reached 118 to 120 GFLOP/s in the profile, at one core's peak. In the library, with packing and the transposed output copy, it reaches 93 to 110.
- **Vectorization is fragile.** With `mul_add`, most other tile shapes fell to 16 to 33 GFLOP/s (4 × 8, 8 × 8, 6 × 8, 12 × 4, 8 × 4, 8 × 16); only 4 × 16 is fast without packing the weights. The tile is chosen by measurement, and the kernel rows in the record show a regression after a compiler change.
- **A single long sum cost accuracy.** With one sequential fused sum per element, the harness passed but the worst error against the reference rose to 6.51e-4 in logits (65% of S-1) and 4.53e-5 in intermediates (45% of S-2): more rounding than the old 16 partial sums, over up to 1536 products. Summing in chunks of 64 and adding the chunk sums in order cost no measurable speed in the library and brought the error below 0.4.1's. The owner chose it before the release's measurements were taken.
- **A guard stopped the first benchmark, as designed.** The prefill sweep refuses to record unless its two paths agree on the last logits; the bound was 1e-5 from the release in which they were bit-identical, and the paths now differ by 1.7e-5. The first run stopped with nothing recorded, and the bound is now the batched-against-one-token bound of 1e-3.
- **The equality rule can change without losing what matters.** Bit-identity with the previous release is gone; what stays is the oracle's judgement, a bounded drift check against the retained previous kernel, exact independence from the thread count, and run-to-run determinism.

## Negative results and open points

- **The default thread count flips between 8 and 12.** The rule (highest decode speed at 512 tokens; counts within 2% tie and the faster prompt wins) picked 12 on one run and 8 on the next: 12's decode is 1.7% off the best in one and 2.7% off in the other, at the band's edge, so it is noise that decides. 12 has the faster prompt by about 10% (604 against 674 ms at 644 tokens) and decode about 1% to 3% slower; they are otherwise equivalent. The release ships 12 (the rows are at 12); 8 is one constant away, and the 8-thread rows are in the record too.
- **Prompt speed gains less with threads than the kernel alone suggests.** The kernel is 2.6× to 2.9× the old loop, one thread gains 1.8× to 2.4× on a prompt and 12 threads about 1.5×. The products are about 80% of a long prefill and the rest (attention, which grows with the square of the prompt, the small operations, the input packing and the transposed output copy) is untouched. These shares are from the profile and the kernel's rate, not measured in the engine.
- **The kernel reaches about the rate of sgemm and no more.** One core's peak is 111 GFLOP/s and the kernel is at 93 to 110 including its copies; there is nothing left in this kernel for a further technique to buy except removing the copies.
- **Decode is the next limit.** It gains 1.66× at most from threads and nothing from this release (Requirements §2.2: reading weights at lower precision, hand-overs in the pool).
- **Not measured: another machine.** The rates rest on one machine and on aarch64's hardware fused multiply-add; on a machine without one, `mul_add` is a slow library call (FR-47, S-7). The output's independence from the thread count rests on dividing only whole output elements.
